    Driving Agricultural Transformation

    India’s agriculture and allied sectors are witnessing a significant transformation, marked by rising
    productivity, stronger farmer support systems, and greater resilience. Agriculture remains the backbone of
    India's economy, providing livelihoods to approximately 46.1 percent of the workforce. Livestock and
    fisheries have maintained stable growth of 5-6 percent, strengthening rural incomes and supporting
    agricultural diversification. Over the past decade, sustained policy interventions have improved access to
    credit, insurance, and markets, and accelerated the adoption of digital technologies. Growing investments
    in food processing, cooperatives, livestock, dairy, and fisheries are enabling greater value addition, income
    diversification, and employment across the agricultural value chain.

    Agricultural Diversification and the Growth of Allied Sectors

    Livestock and Dairy as Drivers of Rural Economic Growth

    The livestock sector has recorded a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 12.77 percent since 2014-
    15. In the Union Budget 2026-27, Rs.6,153.46 crore has been allocated to the Department of Animal
    Husbandry and Dairying.

    Indigenous and non-descript cattle productivity reached 1,343.2 kilograms per animal per year in
    2024-25 from 927 kilograms per animal per year in 2014-15.
    Buffalo productivity reached 2,365.2 kilograms per animal per year in 2024-25 from 1880 kilograms
    per animal per year in 2014-15.
    Average bovine milk productivity has reached 2,251 kilograms per animal per year in 2024-25 from
    1,648 kg per animal per year in 2014-15.




    India holds the leading global position in milk production, accounting for nearly 25 percent of global
    output.

    Per capita milk availability reached 485 grams per day in 2024-25, compared to the global average of 394
    grams per day.



    Milk production has increased by 70 percent, rising from 146.31 million tonnes in 2014-15 to 248 million
    tonnes in 2024-25.

    Advancing Livestock Breeding, Productivity, and Animal Health

    A total of 47,687 Multi-Purpose Artificial Insemination Technicians in Rural India (MAITRIs) have
    been trained, 24 IVF laboratories established, 48 semen stations sanctioned, and 28 heifer rearing centres
    approved under RGM as on 4th August 2026.
    During the last five years, over 17.27 crore artificial inseminations have been carried out under the
    Nationwide Artificial Insemination Programme, benefiting nearly 5.98 crore farmers.
    Accelerated Breed Improvement programme: As on 4th August 2026, 7,959 embryos transferred, 1,666
    pregnancies established, and 1,223 calves born. Out of which 1,142 of the calves are females.

    Strengthening Animal Health and Risk Mitigation

    Livestock productivity and income stability are closely linked to disease control. Under

    National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP)

    As of March 2026, 132.49 crore doses of the Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD) vaccine have been
    administered.
    Brucellosis Control Programme: As of March 2026, 3.17 crores, 4-8 months old bovine female calf for
    Brucellosis have been vaccinated in the country since inception. 296.74 lakh vaccine doses administered
    to eligible female calves to prevent brucellosis and strengthen livestock health during the last five years
    alone.

    Mobile Veterinary Units (MVUs)

    4,019 MVUs are operational across 29 States/UTs, delivering doorstep veterinary services through the toll-
    free number 1962 as of July 2026.
    Benefited 119.77 lakh farmers and treated 245.05 lakh animals, significantly boosting farmer confidence
    and supporting the livestock sector as a viable enterprise.
